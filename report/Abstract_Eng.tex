This report presents the fine-tuning and evaluation of two pretrained Transformer models, UmBERTo and XLM-RoBERTa, for Named Entity Recognition (NER) in Italian, using the NERMuD 2023 dataset. The work was developed as part of the Natural Language Processing course of the Master's programme in Computer Scienece, under the supervision of Fabio Tamburini, at University of Bologna.

The study compares an Italian-specific model (UmBERTo) with a multilingual model (XLM-RoBERTa) to investigate whether language-specific pretraining provides an advantage over multilingual pretraining for Italian NER. Both models were fine-tuned under comparable experimental conditions: the same data splits, the same effective batch size, the same learning rate, and the same evaluation procedure. The evaluation was performed at the entity level using precision, recall, and F1-score, computed with the \texttt{seqeval} library \cite{seqeval}.

The results show that UmBERTo outperforms XLM-RoBERTa on the test set, with a global F1-score of 0.8945 against 0.8743. The advantage of the Italian-specific model is consistent across all three entity types (person, organization, and location) and across two of the three domains (Wikinews and the ADG domain). The only exception is the fiction domain, where XLM-RoBERTa obtains a slightly higher F1-score, suggesting that the benefit of language-specific pretraining depends on the type of text being processed. The report also discusses the limitations of the study, including the absence of statistical significance testing and the presence of factors beyond language coverage that may contribute to the observed differences.
